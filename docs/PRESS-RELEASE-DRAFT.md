## Arizona Mortgage Consultant Launches a Retro Video Game That Puts First-Time Homebuyers’ Faces in the Story

**Erik Miller’s “The First Key” turns budgeting, loan shopping, and home inspections into a free 10-minute pixel-art adventure, with an arcade and a photo-to-pixel avatar built in**

PHOENIX, Ariz., [DATE] — While the White House was adding an arcade to its website this month, an Arizona mortgage consultant was doing something similar for a different reason: helping renters figure out how to buy a home.

Erik Miller, a mortgage consultant with Patriot Home Mortgage (NMLS #263103), today released The First Key, a free browser game at erikmillerhlt.com/play. Players explore the pixel-art town of Hearthvale, weigh two homes against a monthly budget, survive a surprise repair, and earn their “first key” while learning the questions real buyers should ask. The numbers are fictional. The lessons are sourced from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, the Department of Veterans Affairs, and Arizona’s own down payment assistance programs.

Two features set it apart from the average mortgage calculator. Players can upload a photo and the game turns it into a pixel-art portrait on the spot, entirely in the browser, so they walk through Hearthvale as themselves. And a four-game arcade lets them earn coins that become a bigger pretend down payment, then shows them exactly what that does to a monthly payment.

“Nobody wakes up excited to learn what a Loan Estimate is,” said Miller. “But people will spend ten minutes catching coins for an emergency fund or spotting a roof leak in a cutaway house. By the end they’ve practiced the exact decisions that trip up first-time buyers, and they have a written plan they can send me if they want a real conversation. If they don’t, that’s fine too. The game never asks for anything to play.”

Miller appears in the game as the only non-fictional character: the lender at the castle gate, under a Patriot Home Mortgage flag. A hidden portal previews a planned second series about reading and repairing credit reports, and the game teases four future episodes covering pre-approval, making an offer, the under-contract period, and closing day.

The First Key is free, requires no sign-up, works on phones, and is licensed for Arizona buyers. The arcade games can be played and shared individually at erikmillerhlt.com/play/arcade.
